# Daradara Story Shorts

Create an original short from abstract benchmark patterns. Never copy a source
story, transcript, character likeness, shot sequence, footage, voice recording,
music, or sound effect.

## Load References

- Read [channel-analysis.md](references/channel-analysis.md) for the measured
  narrative-channel patterns and the `PBFoxP1hWuM` timeline.
- Read [ranking-analysis.md](references/ranking-analysis.md) when producing a
  TOP-N compilation or comparing ranking hooks.
- Read [language-patterns.md](references/language-patterns.md) before writing
  Japanese or adapting the script into another language.

## Choose One Format

Choose exactly one primary format before scripting:

1. **Narrative 3D story**
   - Target 70-115 seconds.
   - Use one protagonist, one conflict, one reversal, and one aftershock.
   - Prefer generated or user-owned 3D scenes.
2. **TOP-N ranking**
   - Target 20-50 seconds.
   - Use three to six complete micro-events.
   - Use only owned, licensed, permission-granted, Creative Commons, or
     public-domain clips.

Do not combine both formats unless the user explicitly requests a hybrid.

## Apply The Rights Gate

Record the source URL, creator, rights status, evidence, check date, and credit
for every third-party asset. Reject unknown or platform-only rights.

Treat public YouTube availability as research access, not reuse permission.
When rights are unclear, rebuild the beat with original animation.

## Write The Narrative

Use this beat map for a 90-105 second Japanese-first story:

| Time | Function | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| 0-2 s | Consequence hook | Reveal an alarming result before explaining it. |
| 2-15 s | Incident | Name the protagonist, antagonist, and concrete harm. |
| 15-30 s | Failed help | Show why authority, friends, or evidence fails. |
| 30-45 s | Point of no return | Add a second incident or irreversible accusation. |
| 45-65 s | Quiet counterplan | Let the protagonist prepare, investigate, or train. |
| 65-85 s | Confrontation | Echo an earlier phrase and reverse its meaning. |
| 85-100 s | Fallout | Show the cost, apology, departure, or changed status. |
| Final 1-3 s | Moral aftertaste | End on one restrained sentence, not a lecture. |

Keep one new fact per sentence. Alternate setup and escalation. Insert a
specific visual fact every 5-8 seconds: a number, object, injury, deadline,
recording, message, or physical clue.

Use at most two short dialogue lines. Make the second line echo or invert the
first. Avoid graphic violence, unsupported accusations, and sensational claims
that the visuals cannot establish.

## Direct The Characters

Use readable mobile-scale acting:

| Beat | Face | Body and hands |
|---|---|---|
| Threat | Brows down, chin lowered, asymmetrical smirk | Point, crowd personal space, square shoulders |
| Fear | Wide eyes, parted lips, raised inner brows | Pull elbows inward, lean back, protect torso |
| Disbelief | One brow raised, mouth slightly open | Open palms, small head tilt |
| Shame | Eyes down, compressed lips | Rounded shoulders, hands close to body |
| Resolve | Focused eyes, closed mouth | Chin level, fists relaxed, feet planted |
| Reveal | Eyes widen for 4-8 frames | Stop motion briefly, then turn or step back |
| Relief | Softer eyelids, small smile | Drop shoulders, open hands |

Keep the benchmark-inspired rendering intentionally simple: narrow faces,
large glossy eyes, smooth waxy skin, thin limbs, stiff poses, basic clothing
textures, flat daylight, and modest depth of field. Do not chase polished game
cinematics.

## Build The Shot Plan

For narrative mode:

- Cut on nearly every sentence or clause.
- Hold close-ups for 1.2-2.2 seconds.
- Hold medium action shots for 1.8-3.2 seconds.
- Hold establishing shots for 3-5 seconds.
- Use hard cuts for most transitions.
- Use a 2-4% slow push when the image is otherwise static.
- Reserve punch zooms for the hook, clue, reversal, and final reaction.
- Leave 4-8 frames of stillness before the biggest reveal.

Use this visual mix as a starting point:

- 45% close-up faces or hands
- 35% medium two-person action
- 20% wide location or group reaction

Place a two-line persistent premise banner in the upper safe area only when it
does not cover faces. Place narration captions around the lower-middle region
in yellow or white with a thick black outline. Keep each caption to one phrase.

## Design Audio

For Japanese narrative mode:

- Start with a clear synthetic female narrator similar in function, not cloned
  identity, to a neutral urgent VOICEVOX delivery.
- Keep pitch steady and emotional modulation restrained.
- Use 0.05-0.20 second phrase gaps and no long intro silence.
- Keep narration around the language rate in
  [language-patterns.md](references/language-patterns.md).
- Keep one low-volume suspense or emotional music bed under the whole story.
- Duck music 4-7 dB under dialogue and another 2-4 dB at key lines.

Use 6-10 intentional effects per 90 seconds, not one on every cut:

1. Opening hit at 0.0-0.3 s.
2. Soft whoosh on the first identity reveal.
3. Low pulse when authority fails.
4. Short impact or object sound at the point of no return.
5. Riser across the preparation montage.
6. Music drop or 0.2-0.5 s near-silence before confrontation.
7. One restrained impact on the reversal.
8. Low tail or room tone under the final sentence.

Do not claim this effect count is an exact transcription of a benchmark video.
Verify exact effects only from user-owned or licensed source audio.

## Localize Instead Of Translating Literally

Write the Japanese master first when Japan is the target market. Rebuild each
adaptation to the target speaking rate and idiom. Preserve these functions:

- consequence-first hook
- concrete nouns and numbers
- short causal connectors
- one repeated dialogue phrase
- reveal in the final third
- quiet final sentence

Do not preserve identical word order, line breaks, or jokes across languages.
Generate a separate timed script for each language.

## Deliver The Package

Produce:

- original script with time ranges
- scene list with character, expression, pose, camera, and duration
- caption text and safe-area placement
- voice profile and speaking rate
- music and sound-effect cue sheet
- source-rights manifest
- 1080x1920, 30 fps render specification

Before rendering, confirm that the story and visuals are independently created
and that every third-party asset passes the rights gate.

## Complete Japanese Example

The following is an original structural example, not a rewrite of a benchmark
story.

**Title:** 全校放送で親友の嘘を暴いた話

**0:00-0:06**

卒業式の日、学校で一番の優等生が、全校生徒の前で謝りました。

半年間、親友を泥棒に仕立てていたからです。

**0:06-0:15**

始まりは修学旅行の集金袋でした。

十二万円が消え、空の封筒がナオのロッカーから見つかったんです。

**0:15-0:24**

親友のカイは先生に言いました。

「昼休み、教室に残っていたのはナオだけです」

成績も人望もあるカイの言葉を、先生はすぐ信じました。

**0:24-0:34**

ナオが何度否定しても、クラスは目を合わせません。

先生は「返せば大ごとにしない」と繰り返し、ナオは部活動まで
休まされました。

**0:34-0:43**

ですが、ナオは封筒の端についた赤い塗料に気づきます。

同じ色を使っていたのは、体育館の舞台係だけでした。

**0:43-0:55**

その夜、ナオは文化祭のリハーサル映像を一つずつ確認しました。

犯人の顔は映っていません。

ですが、黒いピアノの蓋に教室の入口が反射していたんです。

**0:55-1:05**

そこには集金袋を持ったカイが映っていました。

さらに数分後、空の封筒をナオのロッカーへ入れる姿まで残って
いました。

**1:05-1:15**

ナオは映像を先生に渡しませんでした。

卒業式の朝、カイに一枚の写真を送りました。

「放送室で待ってる」

**1:15-1:25**

全校放送が始まると、カイは震える声で話し始めました。

ナオが奨学金に選ばれたことが悔しくて、学校から追い出そうとした
と認めたんです。

**1:25-1:34**

先生もクラスもナオに謝りました。

ですがナオが最後に言ったのは、責める言葉ではありませんでした。

**1:34-1:39**

「最初から一度だけ、僕の話も聞いてほしかったです」
